# Recall (Come)

Status: **v0** founder draft. Measurement: cue progression. Category: foundation. Safety weight: 1.0 (highest). Minimum age: 8 weeks. Typical time to mastery: 3–6 months.

## Principles

- Follows the three D's. Advance only at ≥ 80% (8/10) at the current level. Increase one D at a time.
- When moving to a more distracting environment, cut distance by 50% first (PM-012).
- Recall MUST always pay: every response rewarded through Stage 3; jackpots for fast responses from Stage 3 on.
- Never call the dog to something it dislikes (bath, crate, end of play) during acquisition.

## Stages

| # | Name | Distance | Duration | Distraction | Environment | Advance when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Close indoor (cue loading) | 5 ft | immediate | none | one quiet room | 80% (8/10) over 3 sessions |
| 2 | Mid indoor | 10 ft, different rooms | immediate | none | indoor, handler stationary | 80% over 3 sessions |
| 3 | Extended indoor | 15 ft, long line as safety | immediate | low (toy on floor, handler moving) | indoor | 80% over 3 sessions |
| 4 | Mild distraction | reset to 5 ft, rebuild to 15 ft | immediate | medium (another person, moderate noise, food present but inaccessible) | indoor | 80% over 3 sessions |
| 5 | Outdoor generalization | start 5 ft, rebuild to 20 ft+ | immediate | high (outdoor, long line) | yard then quiet outdoor | 80% in 3 distinct outdoor environments |

Off-leash reliability beyond Stage 5 is out of MVP (requires socialization prerequisites and advanced distraction mapping).

## Milestones

| Milestone | Stage | Confidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Reliable indoor recall | end of 3 | 75 |
| Recall under distraction | end of 4 | 75 |
| Outdoor recall on long line | end of 5 | 80 |

## Exercise template (Stage 2 example)

- Parameters: distance 10 ft, reps 8–10, environment indoor, distraction none.
- Setup: quiet room, high-value treats in pouch, dog not just fed.
- Steps: stand ~10 ft away; say the dog's name once; give the recall cue once in a happy tone; when the dog arrives, mark and reward at your body; release; repeat.
- Success: dog arrives within ~3 seconds on the first cue in 8 of 10 reps.
- Common mistakes: repeating the cue; rewarding before the dog reaches you; calling when the dog is clearly engaged elsewhere; moving toward the dog.
- If struggling: halve the distance; raise reward value; end on an easy success.

## Coaching guidance

| Problem | Response |
|---|---|
| Dog distracted | Reduce environmental distractions; drop back one D |
| Slow response / hesitation | Increase reward value; shorten distance; add movement away from dog |
| Comes partway and stops | Reward at your body only; run backward as dog approaches |
| Ignores cue outdoors after indoor success | Expected (PM-012); reset distance, use long line, lower distraction |
| Owner execution check | Cue said once? Reward delivered at handler? Tone upbeat? |
